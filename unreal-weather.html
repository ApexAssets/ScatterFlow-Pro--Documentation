ScatterFlow documentation / Unreal plugin

Unreal: Weather

Add precipitation, wet surfaces, puddles, and snow cover

Beta The ScatterFlow plugin for Unreal Engine is in early development. Features, settings, and interface elements may change between releases.

Weather

ScatterFlow Weather adds rain or snow around the camera. It also makes the scene wet, adds puddles, or covers it in snow, without you editing any materials.

If ScatterFlow Sky is in the level, it reacts to the weather. Rain brings darker light and more clouds, snow brings pale clouds and haze, and thunderstorms add lightning.

Start and stop the weather

Click the rain-cloud icon beside Select a layer to edit it to start rain. The first time, ScatterFlow builds the weather assets it needs.

To stop it, click the None button in the Weather section. The sky goes back to its Sky Preset.

Weather Presets

Click a preset button, such as Light Rain, Thunderstorm, or Blizzard, to set the precipitation, surfaces, and sky in one step. Pick the closest one, then fine-tune it.

The settings shown depend on the weather: rain settings for rain, snow settings for snow.

Weather Influence

Sets how much the weather changes the sky. 1 is the normal amount, and 2 makes it stronger. At 0, it still rains or snows, but the sky stays as it is in fair weather.

The fair-weather clouds come from the Fair Weather Clouds slider in the Sky section's Clouds panel. This is also the sky you see before and after the weather.

Transition

Transition (s) sets how many seconds a weather change takes during gameplay. Clouds and wetness build up gradually, and switching to None dries the scene the same way.

In the editor, changes usually appear at once.

Falling rain

Opacity sets how visible the drops are.
Drop Width and Drop Length set the size of the rain streaks.
Fall Speed sets how fast the drops fall.
Particle Count is the number of drops around the camera at full intensity, up to 100,000.
Intensity sets how heavy the rain is. It affects both the falling drops and how wet surfaces get.

The drops are easiest to judge against a dark background.

Follow Wind

Sets how much rain and snow are blown by the level's Wind. At 0, they fall straight down whatever the wind. At 2, the wind has twice the effect.

Splashes

Turn on the Splashes checkbox to add small splashes where raindrops hit the ground. Splash Amount sets how many there are.

Turning Splashes off keeps the ripple rings in puddles.

Wet Surfaces and Darkening

Wet Surfaces makes surfaces darker and glossier and adds puddles on flat ground, without changing your materials. Turn it off to keep the ground dry while it rains.

Darkening sets how much wet surfaces darken. At 0, they only get glossier. At 1, they look soaked.

Puddles

Puddles sets how much flat ground is covered by water. At 0, there are no puddles. Higher values make larger pools.

Puddle waves

Puddle waves add wind-driven movement to the water in puddles. They are easiest to see in reflections at a low angle, with falling rain and ripple rings turned off.

Puddle Waves sets the wave strength. At 0, the surface is still.
Wave Scale sets the wave size, from small ripples to broad swells.
Wave Speed sets how fast the waves move. At 0, they stop.

Puddle Ripples

Sets the strength of the rings from raindrops landing in puddles. At 0, there are no rings. To see the rings on their own, turn puddle waves off.

Falling snow

Flake Size sets the size of the flakes.
Fall Speed sets how fast they fall. Faster snow looks heavier.
Drift makes the flakes swirl as they fall. At 0, they fall straight down.
Particle Count and Opacity set how many flakes there are and how visible they are.

Snow Cover

Snow Cover adds settled snow on upward-facing surfaces. Turn it off to have falling snow that does not build up.

Max Coverage sets how much is covered. Lower values leave patches, and 1 covers everything.

Max Slope

Sets the steepest slope that can hold snow. Lower it to clear snow from steep hillsides and keep it on flatter ground.

Snow patches

Patches only show when the snow does not cover everything, so lower Max Coverage first.

Cover Noise Scale sets the size of the patches. Lower values give broad drifts, and higher values give small patches.
Cover Edge Detail roughens the edges of the patches. At 0, they are smooth.

Dry Under Cover

Dry Under Cover keeps rain, wetness, and snow out from under roofs, bridges, and dense trees. Turn it off to let the weather reach those areas too.

Cover Area is how far around the camera shelter is checked, in centimetres. Shelter further away is ignored.

Instant In Editor

On by default, it shows the finished weather straight away in the editor. Turn it off to preview the gradual changes you get in gameplay, such as wet ground slowly being covered by snow.
