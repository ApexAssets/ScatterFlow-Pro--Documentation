ScatterFlow documentation / Unreal plugin

Unreal: Weather

Add precipitation, wet surfaces, puddles, and snow cover

Beta The ScatterFlow plugin for Unreal Engine is in early development. Features, settings, and interface elements may change between releases.

Weather

ScatterFlow Weather creates rain or snow around the camera and applies wetness, puddles, or snow cover across scene materials without configuring each material individually.

When ScatterFlow Sky is present, it responds to the weather: rain darkens the lighting and increases cloud cover, snow adds pale clouds and haze, and thunderstorms add lightning.

Start and stop the weather

Click the rain-cloud icon beside Select a layer to edit it to start rain. On first use, ScatterFlow builds the required weather assets.

Choose None in the Weather section to stop precipitation and return the sky to its Sky Preset.

Weather Presets

Choose None, Light Rain, Rain, Thunderstorm, Light Snow, Snow, or Blizzard. Each preset configures precipitation, surface effects, and sky conditions together. Start with the closest preset, then refine it.

The visible controls change with the selected weather: rain settings for rain and snow settings for snow.

Weather Influence

Controls how strongly the weather changes the sky. At 1, the normal weather response is applied. At 0, precipitation continues without changing the fair-weather sky. A value of 2 intensifies the sky response.

At 0, cloud coverage comes from Fair Weather Clouds in the Sky section's Clouds panel. This defines the dry-sky conditions before and after the weather.

Transition

Transition (s) sets how long a weather change takes during gameplay. In the editor, changes normally appear immediately.

For example, an 8-second transition gradually increases cloud cover and surface wetness when switching to Rain. Selecting None transitions back to dry conditions.

Falling rain

Use a dark background to inspect raindrop shape and visibility.

Opacity: drop visibility.
Drop Width and Drop Length: the dimensions of the rain streaks.
Fall Speed: how quickly drops descend. Lower values produce slower motion.
Particle Count: the number of drops around the camera at full intensity, up to 100,000.
Intensity: overall rainfall strength, affecting both falling drops and surface wetness.

Follow Wind

Controls how strongly precipitation follows the level's Wind. At 0, rain and snow fall vertically regardless of wind. A value of 2 doubles the directional response.

Splashes

Splashes adds small crown-shaped impacts where raindrops hit the ground. Disabling it removes these impacts but leaves puddle ripple rings. Splash Amount controls their number.

Wet Surfaces and Darkening

Wet Surfaces: enables darker, glossier surfaces and puddles in flat areas without editing the underlying materials. Disable it to keep the ground dry while rain continues.

Darkening: how much wet surfaces darken. At 0, wetness changes gloss without darkening; at 1, the surface has a strongly soaked appearance.

Puddles

Puddles controls water coverage on flat ground. At 0, no puddles appear. Increase it to create larger pools.

Puddle waves

Puddle waves add wind-driven surface motion. To inspect them separately, temporarily disable falling rain and impact rings and view reflections at a low angle.

Puddle Waves: wave strength. At 0, the surface remains still; higher values disturb the reflections.
Wave Scale: the size of the waves, from smaller ripples to broader swells.
Wave Speed: how quickly waves move. At 0, their motion stops.

Puddle Ripples

Controls the rings created by raindrop impacts. At 0, the rings are disabled. Increase it for a stronger effect. Disable puddle waves when you need to inspect the rings on their own.

Falling snow

Flake Size: the size of individual flakes.
Fall Speed: how quickly snowflakes fall. Increase it for faster, heavier-looking snowfall.
Drift: swirling motion during descent. At 0, flakes fall straight down.
Particle Count and Opacity: the number and visibility of flakes.

Snow Cover

Snow Cover: applies settled snow to upward-facing surfaces. Disable it to show falling snow without accumulation on the environment.

Max Coverage: the proportion covered. Lower values leave patches; 1 creates full coverage.

Max Slope

Sets the steepest surface that can retain snow. Lower values remove snow from steep hillsides while preserving it on flatter areas.

Snow patches

Use partial coverage, such as Max Coverage 0.35, to expose the patch pattern.

Cover Noise Scale: patch frequency. Lower values create broader drifts; higher values create smaller patches.
Cover Edge Detail: boundary irregularity. At 0, edges are smooth; higher values add fine detail.

Dry Under Cover

Dry Under Cover: excludes precipitation, wetness, and snow beneath roofs, bridges, and dense tree cover. Disable it to apply weather effects in those areas as well.

Cover Area: the distance around the camera over which shelter is evaluated, in centimetres. Shelters beyond this range are not included.

Instant In Editor

Enabled by default, this shows the completed weather state immediately in the editor. Disable it to preview the gradual changes used during gameplay, such as wet ground slowly becoming snow-covered.
