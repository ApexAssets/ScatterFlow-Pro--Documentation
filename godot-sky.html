Godot: Sky

Control time of day, sunlight, moonlight, clouds, fog, and exposure from one sky node

How the sky works

SF Sky handles the sky, sun, moon, clouds, stars, fog, and exposure in one node. The sun and moon move with the time, place, and season you set.

While the sky is active, it uses its own environment and lights. Your scene's WorldEnvironment and directional lights are switched off, and come back when you remove the sky. The sky's lights are not saved as permanent scene objects.

Adding the sky

Add Sky

Click the sun icon at the top of the ScatterFlow Pro dock to add an SF Sky node to the open scene. Its settings are in the dock's Sky section.

A scene can have only one SF Sky.

Time and place

Time

  • Default 10:00 AM
  • Range 0:00 to 24:00

Sets the time of day. You can drag the slider or type a time, such as 14:30, 2:30 pm, or 9am. The sun, shadows, and sky colours follow it.

Turn on Animate to let time pass while the game runs. Day Length sets how many real minutes a full day takes. In the editor, the time stays where you set it.

Sun Direction

  • Default 0°
  • Range -180° to 180°

Turns the sun's whole path around the scene. Use it to choose where the sun rises and sets, and which way shadows fall, without changing the time. The moon and stars turn with it.

At 0°, the sun rises toward +X, which counts as east.

Latitude and Day of Year

  • Latitude default 40°, -89° to 89°
  • Day of Year default 172, 1 to 365

Latitude sets how far north or south of the equator the scene is. It changes how high the sun climbs and how long twilight lasts. Negative values are in the southern hemisphere.

Day of Year sets the season. Day 172 is midsummer in the northern hemisphere, and day 355 is midwinter.

Moon Age

  • Default 12 days
  • Range 0 to 29.5 days

Sets the number of days since the new moon. About 7 days gives a half moon, and 15 a full moon.

Moon Age also changes where the moon is in the sky and when it rises.

Sky presets

Clear, Partly Cloudy, Cloudy, Overcast, Foggy

Each preset button sets the clouds and fog together. Partly Cloudy is the default.

Clear has no clouds or fog. Partly Cloudy, Cloudy, and Overcast each add more cloud, up to a solid grey layer. Foggy has thick fog and no clouds.

Choosing a sky preset also stops any rain or snow. Fine-tune the result with the settings below.

Clouds

The Clouds panel

The clouds are 3D and lit by the sun. Start with a preset such as Cloudy, then adjust them here.

Density sets how thick and opaque the clouds are. Scale sets their overall size.

Base Altitude sets the height of the cloud bases. Lower clouds sit closer to the scene. Thickness sets how tall the cloud layer is above its base.

Speed and Direction set how the clouds drift. Change sets how quickly their shapes evolve. At 0, they keep their shape as they move.

Quality (steps) sets the rendering detail. Lower values are faster but can look grainy. Higher values are smoother but cost more.

Light

The Light panel

These settings control how bright, what colour, and how large the sun, moon, and stars are.

Sun Intensity sets the strength of the sunlight, and Sun Color tints it. Sun Size sets how large the sun looks. The real sun is about 0.5° across.

Haze adds atmospheric haze. Raise it for a paler sky and softer distances. Sky Brightness sets how bright the sky is and how much it lights the scene.

The Shadows checkbox turns on shadows from the sun and moon.

Moon Intensity, Moon Color, and Moon Size set the strength, tint, and size of the moonlight. Stars sets how bright the stars are. At 0, they are hidden.

Fog and exposure

The Fog & Exposure panel

Fog Density sets the fog in clear weather. Small values blend distant objects into the sky. Weather Fog is the extra fog used by the Foggy preset and in rain or snow.

Auto Exposure is on by default and adjusts the brightness for day, night, and storms. Turn it off to use a fixed exposure set by Exposure.

Exposure adjusts brightness in stops, on top of Auto Exposure when it is on. -1 halves the brightness and +1 doubles it.

Weather Link

The sky follows the weather

Weather can change the clouds, brightness, and fog, and thunderstorms add lightning. Click the None button in the Weather section to go back to your sky preset.

Follow Weather is on by default. Turn it off to keep your sky as it is while rain or snow still falls. Influence sets how strongly the sky reacts to rain or snow.

Transition sets how long the sky takes to change when the weather changes in the game. In the editor, changes are immediate. Lightning / min sets how often lightning flashes in a full thunderstorm.

Selecting and removing the sky

Select Sky and Remove Sky

The Select Sky button selects the SF Sky node, so you can edit its settings in the Inspector.

The Remove Sky button deletes the sky and brings back the scene's previous environment and lights. Deleting the SF Sky node yourself does the same.
