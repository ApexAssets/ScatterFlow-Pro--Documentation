ScatterFlow documentation / River tab

River

Draw editable rivers, shape their banks, and add flowing water and shoreline assets

Drawing a river Size Water Extra Collision Objects Scatter System SF_River Material Rivers and scatter layers Optimization

The River tab creates a river from a stroke drawn on terrain. ScatterFlow shapes the riverbed in the terrain, adds animated water, and can scatter rocks or other assets along its banks.

The result remains editable. Change the course, width, water settings, or bank scatter as your scene develops.

Drawing a river

Draw River

1. Select the terrain.

2. Click Draw River to activate the drawing tool.

3. Draw the river's course across the terrain in one stroke.

4. Press Tab to finish and generate the river.

The terrain's name appears beside River System at the top of the panel. Hover over the i for a summary of the workflow.

Prepare the terrain first. Apply its scale and rotation with Ctrl + A and provide enough geometry to shape the riverbed. A coarse terrain mesh produces rough banks and a less detailed channel.

Edit River

Click Edit River to modify the strokes. Add another stroke to create a tributary, or select and delete strokes you no longer need. Press Tab to update the river.

Reverse the flow

Water follows the direction of the drawn stroke, from its starting point to its endpoint. Reverse the stroke when the flow runs the wrong way; you do not need to redraw the river.

1. Click Edit River.

2. Select the stroke to reverse. Press A for all strokes, or hover over one and press L to select it.

3. Right-click and choose Switch Direction, also available in the viewport header's Segments menu.

4. Press Tab to apply the change. The river keeps its course and flows in the opposite direction.

Increase Flow Speed temporarily if you need to make the direction easier to inspect.

Size

Open River Properties > River > Size.

River Width, River Height and Terrain Falloff

River Width: the width of the river.

River Height: the water level within the riverbed. Raising it fills more of the channel and increases the area covered by water.

Terrain Falloff: how far terrain reshaping extends from the river. Lower values create steeper banks; higher values produce a broader, gentler valley.

Control Curves, at the top of the River panel, identifies the collection containing the drawn strokes.

Water

These controls shape the animated water surface. Play the timeline to preview the result. Solid shading makes the surface geometry easier to inspect without the water material.

Waves and flow

Wave Strength: wave height. For example, increasing it from 0.07 to 0.5 changes a calm stream into visibly rougher water.

Wave Speed: how quickly the waves change.

Flow Speed: how fast the water moves downstream.

Flow Strength: the strength of the streaking along the flow direction.

Foam Spread: how far foam extends from banks and objects in the water. See Foam.

Separate motion adjustments from material adjustments. Use Solid shading to tune waves and ripples, then switch to Material Preview or Rendered shading to evaluate the water's appearance.

Extra Collision Objects

Ripples around objects

Add collision objects to create disturbances around rocks or other objects in the river.

1. Put the objects in a collection, such as River Boulders.

2. Expand Extra Collision Objects and select the collection in Collider.

The Water panel contains two controls for these disturbances:

Collision Ripples Strength: how high the ripples are. Increase it for more pronounced ripples around collision objects.

Collision Ripples Falloff: the distance the ripples extend from each object. Increasing it from 0.1 m to 1 m spreads the effect over a wider area.

Scatter System

Rocks along the river

The river includes a separate scatter system for assets that should follow its course, such as rocks, pebbles, or reeds.

Scatter Collection: the source objects. For example, use Temperate Rocks from the content pack.

Scatter Amount: the number of instances placed.

Min Distance: the minimum spacing between instances.

Scatter Width: how far placement extends from the river. Lower values concentrate assets near the water; higher values spread them further up the banks.

Scatter Falloff: how quickly coverage decreases toward the edge of the scatter band.

Seed: a different arrangement with the same settings.

Scale: Uniform Scale adjusts all instances, while Scale Min and Scale Max define random size variation.

Rotation: Align To Surface follows the terrain slope, while Rotation and Random Rotation control orientation.

Offset: shifts the scattered instances, for example to embed them slightly in the ground.

Foam around scattered objects requires Realize All Instances. Keep this enabled when you need that interaction. It increases processing cost, so use a modest Scatter Amount while editing a long river.

SF_River Material

Open SF_River Material below River Properties to adjust the water's appearance. These changes are visible in Material Preview and Rendered shading.

Water Material

Water Color: the water tint. Use it to change the overall colour, such as from the pale default to blue.

Water Density: how quickly colour builds through the water. Lower values produce lighter, clearer water; higher values produce a darker, deeper appearance.

Murkiness Color and Water Murkiness: add a cloudy tint that obscures the bottom. A brown tint can create muddy water.

Wave Bump adds fine surface detail with Scale, Detail, Distance, and Strength. Depth controls darkening in deeper areas.

Foam and Caustics

Foam appears along the banks and around objects in the water.

Foam Color and Foam Amount: its colour and coverage.

Foam Scale and Foam Detail: the size and complexity of its pattern.

Foam Bump Strength and Foam Bump Distance: the apparent surface depth of the foam.

Caustics adds moving light patterns in shallow water. Adjust them with Caustic Amount, Caustic Strength, Caustic Speed, and Caustic Scale.

Rivers and scatter layers

River tools and ordinary scatter layers can use the same terrain. The layers follow the reshaped ground, but do not automatically avoid the water.

Scattering on a terrain with a river

Use the terrain as the Emitter and create layers normally. Instances follow the riverbed and banks because the river modifies the terrain itself.

Without a mask, the layer also scatters inside the channel. Use Curve Proximity to keep vegetation out of the water.

Use the appropriate scatter system. The river's Scatter System handles assets that belong in or near the water. Use ordinary scatter layers for the rest of the terrain.

Keep a layer out of the river with Curve Proximity

The river's control curve can also define an exclusion area for scatter layers.

1. Select the layer in the System List and expand Proximity.

2. Enable Curve Proximity and keep Type set to Single Curve.

3. Set Curve to SF_River Curve, located in the Outliner's SF_RiverCurves collection.

4. Keep Behaviour set to Avoid and increase Distance until the channel is clear. Distance is measured from the river's centreline, so use at least half the river width. Add more clearance to keep plants off wet banks.

5. Enable Smooth Transition and adjust Transition to create a gradual boundary along the water.

Repeat this for every layer that should avoid the river. Tributaries added with Edit River remain part of the same curve and are included automatically. For a second, separate river, use Multiple Curves and select a collection containing both curves.

See Culling and Proximity for more detail on these controls.

Optimization

River Resolution and Subdivide Level

River Resolution: the detail of the generated river geometry. Lower it to reduce processing cost on long rivers.

Subdivide Level: adds geometry to the terrain around the river, from 0 to 6. Increase it when banks appear blocky and you cannot add detail to the terrain mesh directly. Each additional level increases the processing cost substantially.
