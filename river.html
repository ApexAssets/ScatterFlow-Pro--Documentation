ScatterFlow documentation / River tab

River

Draw editable rivers, shape their banks, and add flowing water and shoreline assets

Drawing a river Size Water Extra Collision Objects Scatter System SF_River Material Rivers and scatter layers Optimization

The River tab turns a stroke drawn on terrain into a river. It carves the riverbed, adds animated water, and can scatter rocks or other assets along the banks.

Everything stays editable, so you can change the course, width, water, or bank scatter at any time.

Drawing a river

Draw River

Select the terrain, click the Draw River button, and draw the river's course in one stroke. Press Tab to generate the river.

For a quick reminder of the workflow, hover over the i icon next to the River System heading.

Prepare the terrain first. Press Ctrl + A to apply its scale and rotation. The terrain also needs enough geometry to carve the riverbed. A coarse mesh gives rough banks.

Edit River

Click the Edit River button to change the strokes. Add a stroke to make a tributary, or delete strokes you no longer need. Press Tab to update the river.

Reverse the flow

Water flows from the start of the stroke to its end. If it flows the wrong way, reverse the stroke instead of redrawing the river.

Click the Edit River button and select the stroke (A selects all, or hover over one and press L). Right-click and choose Switch Direction, which is also in the viewport header's Segments menu. Press Tab to apply it.

Raising Flow Speed for a moment makes the direction easier to see.

Size

These settings are in the River Properties > River > Size panel.

River Width, River Height and Terrain Falloff

River Width sets how wide the river is. River Height sets the water level in the riverbed. Raise it to fill more of the channel.

Terrain Falloff sets how far the terrain is reshaped around the river. Low values give steep banks, and high values give a wide, gentle valley.

The Control Curves field, at the top of the River panel, shows the collection that holds your strokes.

Water

These settings control the moving water surface. Play the timeline to see them in action.

Waves and flow

Wave Strength sets how high the waves are, from a calm stream to rough water. Wave Speed sets how quickly they change.

Flow Speed sets how fast the water moves downstream. Flow Strength sets how strong the streaks along the flow are.

Foam Spread sets how far foam reaches from the banks and from objects in the water.

Separate motion adjustments from material adjustments. Solid shading shows the surface shape clearly, so tune waves and ripples there. Then switch to Material Preview or Rendered shading to judge how the water looks.

Extra Collision Objects

Ripples around objects

Collision objects make the water ripple around rocks and other objects in the river. Put the objects in a collection, then choose it in the Collider field in the Extra Collision Objects panel.

Two settings in the Water panel control these ripples. Collision Ripples Strength sets how high they are. Collision Ripples Falloff sets how far they spread from each object.

Scatter System

Rocks along the river

The river has its own scatter system for assets that follow its course, such as rocks, pebbles, or reeds.

Scatter Collection sets the objects to scatter, such as rocks from the content pack. Scatter Amount sets how many are placed, and Min Distance sets the minimum space between them.

Scatter Width sets how far from the river the assets spread. Low values keep them close to the water. Scatter Falloff sets how quickly they thin out toward the edge. Seed gives a different arrangement.

In the Scale panel, Uniform Scale sizes all instances, and Scale Min and Scale Max add random variation. In the Rotation panel, Align To Surface follows the slope, and Rotation and Random Rotation set the orientation. Offset shifts the instances, for example to sink them slightly into the ground.

Foam around scattered objects requires Realize All Instances. Keep it on if you want that foam. It is slower, so keep Scatter Amount modest while you edit a long river.

SF_River Material

The SF_River Material panel, below River Properties, controls how the water looks. Changes show in Material Preview and Rendered shading.

Water Material

Water Color tints the water. Water Density sets how quickly the colour builds up. Low values give clear water, and high values give darker, deeper-looking water.

Murkiness Color and Water Murkiness add a cloudy tint that hides the bottom. A brown tint gives muddy water.

Wave Bump adds fine surface detail, set with Scale, Detail, Distance, and Strength. Depth darkens the deeper areas.

Foam and Caustics

Foam forms along the banks and around objects in the water. Foam Color and Foam Amount set its colour and coverage. Foam Scale and Foam Detail set the size and complexity of its pattern. Foam Bump Strength and Foam Bump Distance give it depth.

Caustics adds moving light patterns in shallow water. Adjust them with Caustic Amount, Caustic Strength, Caustic Speed, and Caustic Scale.

Rivers and scatter layers

Scatter layers on the same terrain follow the reshaped ground. They do not avoid the water on their own.

Scattering on a terrain with a river

Choose the terrain in the Emitter field and create layers as usual. Because the river reshapes the terrain itself, the instances follow the riverbed and banks.

Without a mask, plants also land in the channel. Use Curve Proximity to keep them out of the water.

Use the appropriate scatter system. The river's Scatter System is for assets in or near the water. Use ordinary scatter layers for the rest of the terrain.

Keep a layer out of the river with Curve Proximity

The river's curve can mark an area for scatter layers to avoid. In the layer's Proximity settings, turn on Curve Proximity with Type set to Single Curve. Set Curve to SF_River Curve, found in the SF_RiverCurves collection, and keep Behaviour on Avoid.

Raise Distance until the channel is clear. It is measured from the centre of the river, so use at least half the river width, plus a little more to keep plants off wet banks. Turn on Smooth Transition and adjust Transition to soften the edge along the water.

Do this for every layer that should avoid the river. Tributaries are part of the same curve, so they are included. For a second, separate river, set Type to Multiple Curves and choose a collection that holds both curves. See Culling and Proximity for more on these settings.

Optimization

River Resolution and Subdivide Level

River Resolution sets how detailed the river geometry is. Lower it to keep long rivers fast.

Subdivide Level adds geometry to the terrain around the river, from 0 to 6. Raise it if the banks look blocky and you cannot add detail to the terrain itself. Each level makes the river much slower to process.
