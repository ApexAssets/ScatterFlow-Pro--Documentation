ScatterFlow documentation / Decals tab

Decals

Place logos, markings, stains, and other images on surfaces without changing their materials

Adding a decal PBR maps Decal Objects list Surface Snap Decal Properties

The Decals tab places an image on a surface as its own object. Use decals for road markings, signs, cracks, dirt, and other small details.

Decals stay editable, and they never change the surface's own material.

Adding a decal

Choose an image, then place it

Click the + button beside the Decal Objects list, choose an image, and click the Add New Decal button. Supported formats are PNG, JPG, TIFF, EXR, TGA, and WebP. Images with a transparent background give clean edges.

You then place the decal in three clicks: its position on the surface, its size (move the mouse up or down), and its rotation (move it left or right). An arrow shows where it will go and which way it will face.

The current step is shown in the lower-left corner of the viewport. Press Esc or right-click to cancel.

Decals preserve the image's proportions. A wide image makes a wide decal. Decals face outward from whatever surface you place them on, including walls, floors, and rocks.

PBR maps

A decal with normal and roughness maps

To give a decal normal, roughness, or other maps, Ctrl-click all the maps for that material in the file browser. ScatterFlow connects them for you, so a rust stain can have colour, bumps, and shine.

Each map is recognised by the end of its filename:

_color, _albedo, _diffuse, or _basecolor: colour.
_n, _nrm, or _normal: normal.
_rough or _roughness: roughness.
_metal or _metallic: metallic.
_alpha, _opacity, or _mask: transparency.
_emit or _emissive: emission.

Map not recognized? Rename it using one of the supported endings above.

Decal Objects list

Select and hide

The list shows every decal in the scene, named after its image. Select one to edit it in Decal Properties.

The Arrow icon selects the decal in the viewport so you can move it with G. The Eye icon hides or shows it in both the viewport and renders.

Surface Snap

Slide decals over surfaces

The magnet button below the + button turns on Surface Snap. While it is on, a decal you move with G slides over the surface under your cursor and turns to face outward.

This lets you move a decal from the ground onto a wall without fixing its rotation by hand.

Surface Snap also affects other objects. It turns on Blender's face snapping. Turn the magnet off when you are done, or other objects will snap too.

Decal Properties

Select a decal and open the Decal Properties panel. The surface it sits on is shown at the top.

Surface settings

Opacity sets how visible the decal is. Lower it for faded or worn markings.

Emission makes the decal glow, for lit signs or screens.

Metallic and Roughness set how the decal reflects light. Low Roughness gives sharp reflections, like a sticker or wet paint.

Alpha is the transparency taken from the image.

Color Adjustment, Damage, Dirt and Blur

Color Adjustment recolours the decal without editing the image. Hue shifts the colours, and 0.5 leaves them unchanged. Saturation sets how strong they are, and Value sets the brightness.

Damage removes parts of the decal so it looks chipped or peeling. Damage Amount is very sensitive: values from 0.01 to 0.04 go from small chips to heavy damage. Scale sets the chip size, Detail, Roughness, and Lacunarity shape their edges, and Damage Position moves the pattern.

Dirt adds grime. Dirt Amount and Dirt Color set its strength and colour, and Wetness and Bump add gloss and depth. It can be hard to see on small decals.

Blur softens the image. At 1 it becomes a soft patch, useful for faint stains or shadows.
