ScatterFlow documentation / Unreal plugin

Unreal: Sky

Control time of day, lighting, clouds, and fog with one sky actor

Beta The ScatterFlow plugin for Unreal Engine is in early development. Features, settings, and interface elements may change between releases.

Sky

ScatterFlow Sky combines the sun, moon, stars, volumetric clouds, fog, and exposure in one actor. It also responds to ScatterFlow weather, adjusting cloud cover and lighting for rain or snow.

Add the sky from the panel, then use its Sky section to configure the environment.

Add the sky

Deselect any layer and click the sun icon beside Select a layer to edit it. On first use, ScatterFlow creates the required materials and textures. The sky is then added and its settings open.

Existing sun, sky light, atmosphere, clouds, fog, and sky dome actors are disabled to avoid overlapping sky systems. Remove Sky restores them.

Enable Realtime to preview cloud movement. The setting is available in the viewport menu.

Time of Day

Time sets the hour. Drag the value or enter a time such as 6:30 PM or 18.5.

Changing it updates the sun's position, sky colour, lighting, shadows, and cloud illumination. The sky transitions through dawn, daylight, sunset, twilight, and night, with the moon and stars visible after dark.

Sun Direction

Rotates the sun's path around the scene, changing where it rises and sets without changing the time of day. The moon and stars rotate with it.

Use it to choose the direction of the lighting and shadows independently of the time setting.

Latitude

Sets the latitude used by the sky, affecting sun height, day length, and twilight. Adjust it to change the sun's path through the scene.

Watch the sun and shadows while adjusting Latitude to judge the change in lighting.

Animate While Playing and Day Length

Animate While Playing: advances time during gameplay. Disable it to keep the selected time fixed.

Day Length (min): the real-time duration of a complete day cycle. A value of 2 produces a fast cycle useful for previewing changes from afternoon to sunset.

Sky Presets

Choose Clear, Partly Cloudy, Cloudy, Overcast, or Foggy to set clouds, lighting, and fog together. These presets describe dry conditions; configure precipitation on the Weather page.

Clouds: Density and Scale

Density: cloud thickness and opacity. Lower values allow more light through; higher values create denser clouds with darker interiors.

Scale: cloud size. Lower values create smaller formations, while higher values create broader clouds.

Base Altitude

Sets the height of the cloud layer's underside in kilometres. Lower clouds appear closer and occupy more of the view; higher clouds appear further away.

Speed

Sets cloud movement in metres per second. At 0, clouds stop drifting. They move in the direction of the level's wind.

Cloud Shadows

Enables moving shadows from clouds on the terrain. Disable it to remove their influence on direct lighting below.

Sun Intensity

Controls sunlight strength. Automatic exposure compensates for lighting changes, so increasing intensity does not necessarily brighten the entire image. It can instead produce stronger contrast and more pronounced shadows.

Fog Density

Controls distance haze. Higher values obscure distant objects first and progressively affect nearer areas. The fog colour follows the sky and sun.

Volumetric Fog

Allows sunlight to illuminate the fog itself, creating visible light and depth in the atmosphere. It is especially apparent with a low sun shining through the haze.

This option is disabled by default because it adds rendering cost.

Moon and Stars

Moon Intensity (lux): moonlight strength. A value of 0 produces very dark nights; increase it to improve nighttime visibility.

Stars: the brightness of the stars and Milky Way. They become visible after sunset and are obscured by clouds.

Select Sky and Remove Sky

Select Sky: selects the sky actor so you can access its full Details panel. Additional controls include day and night length, day of year, moon phase, cloud colours, and exposure.

Remove Sky: deletes the ScatterFlow sky and restores the level's previous sky setup.

Recorded with ScatterFlow Unreal Sync 2.20 (Sky and Weather: 2.23) in Unreal Engine 5.8, linked to ScatterFlow 1.0.9 in Blender 5.2.2.
