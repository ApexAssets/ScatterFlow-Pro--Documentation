ScatterFlow documentation / Unreal plugin

Unreal: Sky

Control time of day, lighting, clouds, and fog with one sky actor

Beta The ScatterFlow plugin for Unreal Engine is in early development. Features, settings, and interface elements may change between releases.

Sky

ScatterFlow Sky puts the sun, moon, stars, clouds, fog, and exposure in one actor. It also reacts to ScatterFlow weather, so rain and snow change the clouds and lighting.

Add the sky from the panel, then adjust it in the Sky section.

Add the sky

Deselect any layer, then click the sun icon beside Select a layer to edit it. The first time, ScatterFlow creates the materials and textures it needs.

Any sun, sky light, atmosphere, cloud, fog, or sky dome actors already in the level are turned off, so two skies do not overlap. The Remove Sky button turns them back on.

Enable Realtime to preview cloud movement. You can find it in the viewport menu.

Time of Day

Time sets the hour. Drag it, or type a time such as 6:30 PM or 18.5.

The sun, sky colour, lighting, shadows, and clouds all follow the time. The moon and stars come out after dark.

Sun Direction

Turns the sun's path around the scene, so it rises and sets somewhere else. The time of day stays the same, and the moon and stars turn with it.

Use it to choose where the light and shadows come from.

Latitude

Sets the latitude of the scene. It changes how high the sun climbs, how long the day lasts, and how long twilight lasts.

Animate While Playing and Day Length

Animate While Playing moves the time forward during gameplay. Turn it off to keep the time fixed.

Day Length (min) is how many real minutes a full day takes. A short day is handy for previewing the change from afternoon to sunset.

Sky Presets

Click a preset button, such as Clear, Overcast, or Foggy, to set the clouds, lighting, and fog in one step.

All of them are dry. For rain or snow, see Weather.

Clouds: Density and Scale

Density sets how thick the clouds are. Thin clouds let more light through, and thick clouds are darker inside.

Scale sets the size of the clouds, from small formations to broad ones.

Base Altitude

Sets the height of the cloud base, in kilometres. Low clouds look closer and fill more of the sky.

Speed

Sets how fast the clouds drift, in metres per second. They move with the level's wind. Set it to 0 to stop them.

Cloud Shadows

Casts moving cloud shadows on the ground. Turn it off if you do not want the clouds to dim the sunlight below.

Sun Intensity

Sets how strong the sunlight is.

Automatic exposure adjusts for brightness, so a stronger sun does not always make the whole image brighter. It often gives more contrast and deeper shadows instead.

Fog Density

Sets the amount of haze in the distance. Higher values hide far objects first, then nearer ones. The fog takes its colour from the sky and sun.

Volumetric Fog

Lets sunlight light up the fog itself, which adds visible light and depth to the air. It stands out most with a low sun.

It is off by default because it adds rendering cost.

Moon and Stars

Moon Intensity (lux) sets the strength of the moonlight. At 0, nights are very dark. Raise it to see more at night.

Stars sets the brightness of the stars and Milky Way. They appear after sunset and are hidden by clouds.

Select Sky and Remove Sky

The Select Sky button selects the sky actor, so you can use its full Details panel. It has more settings there, such as day and night length, day of year, moon phase, cloud colours, and exposure.

The Remove Sky button deletes the ScatterFlow sky and brings back the level's previous sky.

Recorded with ScatterFlow Unreal Sync 2.20 (Sky and Weather: 2.23) in Unreal Engine 5.8, linked to ScatterFlow 1.0.9 in Blender 5.2.2.
