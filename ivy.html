ScatterFlow documentation / Ivy tab

Ivy

Draw climbing plants on surfaces and control their stems, leaves, flowers, and growth

Adding ivy Ivy Mode Visibility and the Ivy List Changing leaves and flowers Growth Leaves Flowers Wind Optimization Ivy Bake

The Ivy tab grows climbing plants on objects such as walls, trunks, and rocks. You draw the main stems, ScatterFlow adds the vines, branches, leaves, and flowers, and you fine-tune the result in the Ivy Properties panel.

Adding ivy

Choose a preset

A preset gives you a plant's leaves, flowers, and growth settings in one go. The content pack includes everything from green climbers to flowering clematis.

Select a preset in the Asset Browser, under Ivy > Presets in the ScatterFlow_Pro_ContentPack library. Then select your target, click Add Ivy, and draw. The new ivy uses that preset.

A preset is optional. Without one, Add Ivy creates bare stems until you assign leaves. If presets are missing, see Adding the library.

Add Ivy and draw

Select the object to grow on. You can select several for the ivy to grow across. Place the 3D Cursor where the plant should start with Shift + Right Click, then click Add Ivy.

Draw the main stems on the surface. Each stroke is one stem. Press Tab to finish and generate the ivy.

Start with a few simple strokes. Your strokes are the main stems, not every vine. Branches and vines spread from them on their own, so you rarely need to draw much.

Edit Ivy

Select an ivy in the Ivy List and click Edit Ivy to change its strokes. Add stems to cover more, or delete the ones you don't want. Press Tab to regenerate the plant.

Swap the preset

To change an ivy's preset, select a new one in the Asset Browser and click Swap Preset, the circular-arrow button between Leaf Visibility and Flower Visibility. Your stems stay; the leaves, flowers, and growth settings are replaced.

To replace only the leaves or flowers, use the controls described under Changing leaves and flowers.

Ivy Mode

Fast Mode and Realistic Mode

  • Type Two buttons
  • Default Realistic Mode

Realistic Mode grows stems along the surface from your strokes, with vines, branches, leaves, and flowers. Use it for plants seen up close.

Fast Mode skips the stems and just covers the target with leaves and flowers. It is lighter, so it suits distant surfaces or large areas.

The available settings depend on the mode. Each mode has its own settings in Ivy Properties. Ivy Bake only works in Realistic Mode.

Visibility and the Ivy List

Leaf Visibility, Flower Visibility and the list

The Leaf Visibility and Flower Visibility buttons show or hide those parts of the selected ivy. Hide flowers for a plant that doesn't flower, or hide both while you shape the stems.

The Ivy List has one entry for each ivy in the scene. Select one to edit it. In each row, the Eye icon toggles viewport visibility, the Camera icon toggles render visibility, and the Bin icon deletes the ivy along with its stems and collections.

Hover over the i icon above the list for a quick workflow reminder.

Changing leaves and flowers

The two + buttons swap the leaf or flower meshes without changing your stems or growth settings. The left one, beside Leaf Visibility, changes the leaves; the right one, beside Flower Visibility, changes the flowers.

Leaves and flowers from the content pack

Select the ivy in the Ivy List. Then pick a leaf set from Ivy > Leafs in the Asset Browser and click the left +, or a flower set from Ivy > Flowers and click the right +.

You can mix leaves and flowers from different plants.

Use the correct replacement button. Either + accepts whatever asset is selected, so a leaf set can end up as flowers, or a flower set as leaves.

Your own leaves and flowers

Put your leaf or flower objects in a collection. If it holds several objects, the ivy uses a mix of them.

Click an empty area of the Asset Browser to clear its selection. Then select your collection in the Outliner, or an object inside it, and click the left + for leaves or the right + for flowers. The ivy keeps its overall shape.

You can also set a collection directly in Ivy Properties, in the Leaf Source field of the Leafs panel or the Flower Source field of the Flowers panel.

Asset Browser selection takes priority. If an asset is still selected there, the + button uses it instead of your collection. Clear the Asset Browser selection first.
Place origins at the attachment point. Model each leaf or flower at the size you want, with its origin where it meets the stem. Fine-tune it with Leaf Size, Flower Size, and the rotation settings.

Growth

Expand the Ivy Properties panel to edit the selected plant. The ivy Growth panel controls its stems and branches.

Vines and branches

Vines Spread sets how far vines reach from your stems. Raise it to cover more surface without drawing more. Branch Spread sets how far the smaller branches reach from the vines.

Overall Vine Size sets the stem thickness. Vines Surface Offset sets the gap between the stems and the surface.

Under Vines, Vine Density sets the number of vines, Vine Size their thickness, and Vine Seed their arrangement.

Branches has the same controls for branches: Branch Density, Branch Size, and Branch Seed. Leaves and flowers grow on branches, so more branches make a fuller plant.

Use seeds to try different growth patterns. Changing a seed rearranges the growth but keeps your other settings. It helps when a vine grows somewhere you don't want.

Growth in Fast Mode

Fast Mode has no stems to edit, so its ivy Growth panel is simpler.

Spread sets how far the coverage reaches across the target, in metres. Density sets how much of the surface is covered. Max Scale and Min Scale set the size range of the pieces, and Seed gives a different arrangement.

The Leafs, Flowers, Wind, and Optimization panels work in both modes.

Leaves

Leafs

The Leafs checkbox turns leaves on or off, like Leaf Visibility. Leaf Source is the collection the leaves come from.

Leaf Density sets how many leaves grow along the stems, and Leaf Spacing the minimum gap between them. Leaf Seed rearranges them.

Leaf Size Min and Leaf Size Max set the size range. Leaf Rotation and Leaf Rotation2 add random rotation, and Leaf Downward Tilt Min and Max set how far the leaves tilt down.

Flowers

Flowers

The Flowers panel works like Leafs. The Flowers checkbox turns them on, Flower Source sets their collection, and Flower Density, Flower Spacing, Flower Seed, Flower Size Min, and Max control how they are placed.

Under Flower Rotation, Flowers Rotation adds random rotation. Flowers Min and Max Upward Tilt set how far flowers turn up and away from the surface.

Adjust Flower Density gradually. Presets use around 0.01, and 0.05 can already be very dense.

Wind

Wind

Turn on Wind and play the timeline to see the leaves and flowers move.

Global/Wind Strength sets how far they move, and Global/Wind Speed how fast.

Global/Wind Scale sets the size of the gusts. Small values make neighbouring leaves move differently; large values move broad areas together.

Keep ivy wind subtle. Low strength moves the leaves without the whole plant seeming to sway away from the wall.

Optimization

Viewport Density, levels and Advanced

Viewport Density sets how many of the leaves and flowers show in the viewport, from 0 to 1. Lower it to keep a heavy scene responsive while you work.

Viewport Level and Render Level set how many times the growth expands on itself. Each level adds a lot more coverage and takes more processing. Keeping them separate lets you work at level 1 and render a fuller plant.

Under Advanced, Decimate Vines and Decimate Branches simplify the stems, and Vines Resolution sets how round they are. More decimation is lighter but also changes the growth pattern, so set it before you fine-tune the plant.

Increase levels one step at a time. Level 3 can make a very dense plant on a large wall.

Misc and Sources

Misc has finer controls for stem variation, such as Noise Scale, Noise Strength, and Branch Offset. Sources shows the drawn curve, the target objects, and the Vines Material used for the stems.

Ivy Bake

Bake Ivy

Ivy updates whenever something it depends on changes. Baking stores the finished plant so heavy scenes play back faster. The bake controls are at the bottom of Ivy Properties, in Realistic Mode.

Click the Still button to cache the current frame, for ivy that doesn't move, or the Animation button to cache the scene's whole frame range, including wind. Then click Bake Ivy.

The Bin button deletes the bake so the ivy responds to edits again.

Save before baking, and bake after final edits. A baked ivy ignores setting changes until you delete its bake.
