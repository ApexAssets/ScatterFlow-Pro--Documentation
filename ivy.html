ScatterFlow documentation / Ivy tab

Ivy

Draw climbing plants on surfaces and control their stems, leaves, flowers, and growth

Adding ivy Ivy Mode Visibility and the Ivy List Changing leaves and flowers Growth Leaves Flowers Wind Optimization Ivy Bake

The Ivy tab creates climbing plants on objects such as walls, trunks, and rocks. Draw the main stems, and ScatterFlow generates vines, branches, leaves, and flowers along the surface. Use Ivy Properties to refine the result afterward.

Adding ivy

Choose a preset

Presets include a plant's leaves, flowers, and growth settings. The content pack provides options ranging from green climbers to flowering clematis.

1. Open an Asset Browser and select ScatterFlow_Pro_ContentPack.

2. Open Ivy > Presets.

3. Select the preset you want to use.

4. Select the target wall, click Add Ivy, and draw the stems. The new ivy uses the selected preset's assets and settings.

A preset is optional. Without one, Add Ivy creates bare stems until you assign leaves. Select a preset first to start with a complete plant. If presets are missing, see Adding the library.

Add Ivy and draw

1. Select the target object. You can select multiple objects for the ivy to grow across.

2. Position the 3D Cursor at the plant's starting point with Shift + Right Click.

3. Click Add Ivy to activate the drawing tool.

4. Draw the main stems on the surface. Each stroke creates one stem, and you can add multiple strokes.

5. Press Tab to finish and generate the ivy.

Start with a few simple strokes. The strokes define the main stems, not every vine. Branches and vines spread from them automatically, so dense drawing is often unnecessary.

Edit Ivy

Select an entry in the Ivy List and click Edit Ivy to edit its strokes. Add stems to extend coverage, or select and delete unwanted strokes. Press Tab to regenerate the plant.

Swap the preset

Select another preset in the Asset Browser, then click Swap Preset, the circular-arrow button between Leaf Visibility and Flower Visibility. The drawn stems stay in place, while leaves, flowers, and growth settings are replaced.

To replace only the leaves or flowers, use the controls described under Changing leaves and flowers.

Ivy Mode

Fast Mode and Realistic Mode

  • Type Two buttons
  • Default Realistic Mode

Realistic Mode generates stems along the surface from your strokes, with attached vines, branches, leaves, and flowers. Use it when the plant will be viewed closely.

Fast Mode skips the stems and covers the target with leaves and flowers. It has a lower processing cost and suits distant surfaces or large areas.

The available settings depend on the mode. Switching modes changes Ivy Properties. Ivy Bake is available only in Realistic Mode.

Visibility and the Ivy List

Leaf Visibility, Flower Visibility and the list

Leaf Visibility and Flower Visibility toggle those parts of the selected ivy. Hide flowers for a non-flowering plant, or hide both leaves and flowers while shaping the stems.

The Ivy List contains one entry per ivy in the scene. Select an entry to edit it. Each row includes:

Eye: toggles viewport visibility.
Camera: toggles render visibility.
Bin: deletes the ivy, including its stems and collections.

Hover over the i above the list for a brief workflow reminder.

Changing leaves and flowers

The two + buttons replace leaf or flower meshes without changing the drawn stems or growth settings. The left button, beside Leaf Visibility, changes the leaves. The right button, beside Flower Visibility, changes the flowers. Replacing one does not change the other.

Leaves and flowers from the content pack

1. Select the ivy in the Ivy List.

2. Open Ivy > Leafs in the Asset Browser and select a leaf set.

3. Click the left + to replace the leaves.

4. Open Ivy > Flowers, select a flower set, and click the right + to replace the flowers.

You can combine sets from different plants, such as Akebia leaves and Beaumontia flowers on a clematis setup.

Use the correct replacement button. Either + accepts the selected asset. A leaf set can therefore be assigned as flowers, or a flower set as leaves.

Your own leaves and flowers

Place your leaf or flower objects in a collection. If the collection contains several objects, the ivy uses a mix of them.

1. Clear the Asset Browser selection by clicking an empty area.

2. Select your collection in the Outliner, or select an object within it.

3. Click the left + for leaves or the right + for flowers.

For example, a My Leaves collection can contain multiple custom leaf shapes, while My Flowers supplies a separate flower mesh. Replacing these sources preserves the ivy's overall shape.

You can also assign a collection directly in Ivy Properties: use Leaf Source in the Leafs panel or Flower Source in the Flowers panel.

Asset Browser selection takes priority. If an asset remains selected there, the + button uses it instead of your selected collection. Clear the Asset Browser selection before assigning scene objects.
Place origins at the attachment point. Model each leaf or flower at its intended size, with its origin where it joins the stem. Use Leaf Size, Flower Size, and rotation settings to refine its placement on the vine.

Growth

Expand Ivy Properties to access the selected plant's settings. The ivy Growth panel controls its stems and branching.

Vines and branches

Vines Spread: how far vines extend from the drawn stems. Increase it to cover more surface without adding strokes.

Branch Spread: how far smaller branches extend from the vines.

Overall Vine Size: the overall stem thickness.

Vines Surface Offset: the distance between stems and the target surface.

Under Vines, use Vine Density for the number of vines, Vine Size for thickness, and Vine Seed for a different arrangement.

Under Branches, Branch Density, Branch Size, and Branch Seed provide equivalent controls for branches. Leaves and flowers grow on branches, so increasing branch density produces a fuller plant.

Use seeds to try different growth patterns. Changing a seed preserves the other settings while regenerating the arrangement. This is useful when a vine grows into an unwanted position.

Growth in Fast Mode

Fast Mode has no drawn stems to edit. Its ivy Growth panel instead provides:

Spread: coverage distance across the target, in metres.

Density: the amount of surface coverage.

Max Scale and Min Scale: the random size range for the generated pieces.

Seed: a different arrangement using the same settings.

The Leafs, Flowers, Wind, and Optimization panels are available in both modes.

Leaves

Leafs

The Leafs checkbox toggles leaves, like Leaf Visibility. Leaf Source selects the collection used for their meshes.

Leaf Density: the number of leaves along the stems.

Leaf Spacing: the minimum gap between leaves.

Leaf Seed: a different arrangement using the same leaf sources.

Leaf Size Min and Leaf Size Max: the range of leaf sizes.

Leaf Rotation and Leaf Rotation2: random rotation amounts.

Leaf Downward Tilt Min and Max: the range of downward leaf tilt.

Flowers

Flowers

The Flowers panel follows the same pattern as Leafs: the Flowers checkbox enables them, Flower Source selects their collection, and Flower Density, Flower Spacing, Flower Seed, Flower Size Min, and Max control their placement and variation.

Under Flower Rotation, Flowers Rotation adds random rotation. Flowers Min and Max Upward Tilt control how far flowers face upward and away from the surface.

Adjust Flower Density gradually. Presets use values around 0.01; a value of 0.05 can already produce dense flowering.

Wind

Wind

Enable Wind and play the timeline to preview leaf and flower movement.

Global/Wind Strength: how far the leaves and flowers move.

Global/Wind Speed: how quickly the leaves and flowers move.

Global/Wind Scale: the gust size. Smaller values create variation between neighbouring leaves; larger values make broader areas move together.

Keep ivy wind subtle. Use low strength to move the leaves without making the whole plant appear to sway away from the wall.

Optimization

Viewport Density, levels and Advanced

Viewport Density: the proportion of leaves and flowers shown in the viewport, from 0 to 1. Lower it to keep a heavy scene responsive while editing.

Viewport Level and Render Level: how many times growth expands from itself. Higher levels produce substantially more coverage and require more processing. Separate values let you work at viewport level 1 while rendering a more detailed plant.

Under Advanced, Decimate Vines and Decimate Branches simplify the stems, while Vines Resolution controls their roundness. Higher decimation values reduce processing cost but also change the growth pattern, so set them before refining the plant.

Increase levels one step at a time. Level 3 can generate a very dense plant on a large wall.

Misc and Sources

Misc contains finer controls for stem variation, including Noise Scale, Noise Strength, and Branch Offset. Sources identifies the drawn curve, target objects, and Vines Material used for the stems.

Ivy Bake

Bake Ivy

Ivy updates when something it depends on changes. Baking stores the finished plant to improve playback in heavy scenes. The bake controls are at the bottom of Ivy Properties in Realistic Mode.

Still: caches the current frame. Use it for ivy without animation.

Animation: caches the full scene frame range, including wind.

Bake Ivy: starts the bake.

Bin: deletes the cache so the ivy can respond to edits again.

Save before baking, and bake after final edits. A baked ivy does not respond to setting changes until you delete its bake.
