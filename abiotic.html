Abiotic masks

Place instances according to terrain slope, elevation, and facing direction

Slope Mask Elevation Mask Angle Mask

The Abiotic section of the Scatter tab has three masks that place a layer by the shape of the terrain: its slope, its height, and the direction it faces. Each layer has its own masks, and you can combine all three.

These masks hide instances by shrinking them to zero. A smooth transition therefore makes instances smaller near the edge, rather than fewer.

Slope Mask

Keeps a layer on flatter ground, or on steeper ground when inverted. Use it to keep trees on terraces and off cliff faces.

Slope

  • Default 0.85
  • Range 0 to 1

Sets how flat the ground must be for the layer to appear. 1 is level ground and 0 is a vertical wall.

Raise Slope to keep the layer on flatter ground. Lower it to allow steeper slopes. As a guide, 0.85 allows slopes up to about 32°, 0.7 about 45°, and 0.5 about 60°.

Smooth Transition

  • Default off
  • Transition 0.5

Fades instance size across the slope edge. Instances are zero size at the cutoff and reach full size on ground that is flatter by the Transition amount.

A small Transition gives a narrow band of smaller instances. A larger one spreads the band further onto flat ground.

Keep Slope + Transition at or below 1. Ground cannot be flatter than 1, so with a higher total, instances never reach full size. At the defaults of 0.85 and 0.5, even level ground only reaches 30% scale. A Transition of 0.05–0.15 is a good place to start.

Invert

  • Default off

Reverses the mask, so the layer stays on steep ground and off flat ground. With Invert on, lowering Slope keeps the layer on steeper and steeper surfaces.

For example, use an inverted mask for rocks or scree on a cliff, next to a plant layer with the same Slope that is not inverted.

Elevation Mask

Limits a layer by height. Use it for boundaries such as a mountain tree line.

Height

  • Default 5 m

Sets the highest point the layer reaches. Without Smooth Transition, instances above it are removed.

Height is measured along the scene's Z axis, so check where your terrain sits, especially if it is far above or below zero.

Layer disappears after enabling the mask? The default cutoff is 5 m. If your whole emitter is higher than that, raise Height until the layer shows.

Smooth Transition

  • Default off
  • Transition 5

Fades instances out above the cutoff. They stay full size up to Height, then shrink to zero over the next Transition metres.

Use a short transition for a sharp tree line, or a long one for a gradual edge.

Invert

  • Default off

Keeps instances above the cutoff instead of below it, so Height becomes the layer's lower limit.

Use it for high-altitude plants or rocks near a snow line. Two layers with the same Height, one inverted and one not, split a terrain into upper and lower areas.

Randomize

  • Default off
  • Amount 55
  • Scale 0.2

Adds noise to the height cutoff, so the edge does not follow a flat line.

Amount sets how far the cutoff can move. At 0 it does not move. The default of 55 suits large terrains; lower it on smaller terrain, where the effect can be too strong.

Scale sets the size of the pattern. Lower values give broad changes along the edge; higher values give smaller, more frequent ones.

Angle Mask

Limits a layer by the direction a slope faces. Use it for moss on shaded hillsides, or plants on slopes that face the sun.

Angle

  • Default 0°

Sets the compass direction the slope must face, which decides the side of each hill the layer covers.

Without Smooth Transition, instances are largest on steep slopes that face that direction, and get smaller toward the sides. Level ground faces no direction, so it stays empty at the default Offset.

Offset

  • Default 0

Widens or narrows the area the mask covers.

Positive values keep the layer on steeper slopes facing the chosen angle. Negative values spread it further around the hill and onto level ground.

Smooth Transition

  • Default off
  • Transition 0.5

Sets how gradually instances grow from zero to full size across the mask edge.

Use a small Transition for full-size instances with a clear edge. Larger values give a wider, softer band of smaller instances.

Enabling Smooth Transition slightly narrows the mask. Lower Offset to get the previous coverage back.
