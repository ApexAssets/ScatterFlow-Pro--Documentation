Godot: Weather

Add rain and snow, wet surfaces, puddles, and snow cover

Beta ScatterFlow Link for Godot is in early development. Features, settings, and interface elements may change between releases.
How the weather works Starting and stopping the weather General Rain Snow

How the weather works

SF Weather adds rain or snow around the camera and changes the surfaces of the scene to match: wet, darker ground and puddles in rain, settled snow in snowfall. Surface effects apply to the materials ScatterFlow brings over from Blender, so no material needs editing by hand.

With an SF Sky in the scene, the sky responds to the weather: clouds thicken, the light dims, fog comes in, and thunderstorms add lightning. See Weather Link for how strongly the sky follows.

Starting and stopping the weather

Add Weather

Click the rain-cloud icon at the top of the ScatterFlow Pro dock. An SF Weather node is added to the open scene and rain starts falling around the camera.

The dock shows a Weather section with the presets and settings. Choose None to stop the rain or snow; the SF Weather node is removed and the sky returns to its Sky Preset.

Only one SF Weather drives a scene's surfaces at a time.

Weather presets

Choose None, Light Rain, Rain, Thunderstorm, Light Snow, Snow, or Blizzard. Each preset sets the type of precipitation, its intensity and wind, and whether snow settles. Start with the closest preset, then refine it.

Light Rain: light rain under a closing sky.
Rain: steady rain under a low, dark cloud deck.
Thunderstorm: heavy rain, strong wind, and lightning.
Light Snow: light snowfall.
Snow: steady snowfall with snow building up.
Blizzard: heavy snow and wind, close to a white-out.

The settings below the presets change with the weather: rain settings while it rains, snow settings while it snows.

General

Intensity, Wind, and Area

  • Intensity 0.7, 0 to 1
  • Wind 0.25, 0 to 1
  • Wind Direction 0°, -180° to 180°
  • Area 36 m, 8 to 200 m

Intensity: overall strength of the rain or snow. It scales the amount of falling precipitation, and in rain also how wet surfaces become and how often drops ring the puddles.

Wind: how strongly the wind drives the precipitation. At 0, drops and flakes fall straight down; higher values slant them and carry them sideways.

Wind Direction: the direction the wind carries the precipitation, in degrees. At 0°, it blows toward +X.

Area: the size of the region around the camera where rain or snow falls. A smaller area concentrates the particles near the camera; a larger one fills more of a wide view at the same particle count.

Preview in Editor: shows the falling rain or snow in the editor viewport. Disable it to work without the particles; wet surfaces, puddles, and snow cover remain visible, and the precipitation still falls in the running game.

Rain

Falling rain

  • Fall Speed 9.5 m/s
  • Drop Length 0.15 m
  • Drop Width 0.009 m
  • Opacity 0.42
  • Amount 20,000 drops

Use a dark background, such as a wall or a forest edge, to inspect the drops.

Fall Speed: how quickly drops descend, up to 20 m/s. Lower values produce slower motion.
Drop Length and Drop Width: the dimensions of each streak.
Opacity: how visible the drops are.
Amount (drops): the number of drops around the camera at full intensity, from 1,000 to 100,000.
Color: tints the drops. Their brightness also follows the light of the sky, so rain stays dim at night.

Splashes

  • Default on
  • Amount 0.8, 0 to 1

Splashes adds small bursts where raindrops hit the ground. Disable it to remove the bursts while keeping the rain rings on puddles. Amount controls how many splashes appear.

Wet Surfaces

  • Default on
  • Wetness 0.75, 0 to 1
  • Darkening 0.4, 0 to 1

Wet Surfaces: makes ScatterFlow materials darker and glossier in the rain and fills puddles on flat ground. Disable it to keep the ground dry while rain continues to fall.

Wetness: how wet surfaces become. At 0, they stay dry; at 1, they are fully soaked and glossy.

Darkening: how much wet surfaces darken. At 0, wetness changes only the gloss; at 1, the surface takes on a strongly soaked appearance.

Puddles

  • Amount 0.5
  • Waves 0.07
  • Wave Size 1
  • Wave Speed 1
  • Rain Rings 1

Puddles are level, mirror-like water that reflects the sky and the scene. They fill the low spots of a material first, such as the gaps between paving stones, then grow and join as the amount rises. Each puddle has a damp, darker rim.

Amount: how much of the flat ground the puddles cover. At 0, no puddles form.

Waves: strength of the wind-driven waves on the puddles, up to 2. At 0, the water is glass-still.
Wave Size: the size of the waves. 2 makes them twice as large.
Wave Speed: how fast the waves travel. At 0, they stop.

Rain Rings: the expanding rings where drops hit the puddles, up to 2. At 0, the rings are disabled. Light rain produces fewer rings than heavy rain.

Reflections need screen-space reflections. Puddles reflect the scene when the environment has screen-space reflections (SSR) enabled. Without them, puddles reflect only the sky.

Snow

Falling snow

  • Fall Speed 1.1 m/s
  • Flake Size 0.016 m
  • Drift 0.6
  • Opacity 0.9
  • Amount 20,000 flakes

Fall Speed: how quickly flakes fall, up to 5 m/s. Higher values give faster, heavier-looking snowfall.
Flake Size: the size of each flake.
Drift: the side-to-side swirl of the flakes as they fall, up to 3. At 0, they fall straight down.
Opacity: how visible the flakes are.
Amount (flakes): the number of flakes around the camera at full intensity, from 1,000 to 100,000.
Color: tints the flakes.

In strong wind, fast flakes are drawn as short streaks along their motion, and flakes that leave the area around the camera re-enter on the opposite side, so the snowfall stays full in a blizzard.

Snow Cover

  • Amount 0.8
  • Max Coverage 1
  • Patch Size 1
  • Edge Detail 2

Snow Cover: settles snow on upward-facing ScatterFlow surfaces. The snow presets enable it. Disable it to show falling snow without accumulation.

Amount: how much snow has settled, from a light dusting to full cover.

Max Coverage: the largest share of the ground that snow can cover. Lower values leave bare patches; 1 allows full coverage.

Patch Size: the size of the snow patches, from 0.2 to 5. 2 makes them twice as large.

Edge Detail: how ragged the edges of the snow are, up to 3. At 0, the outlines are smooth.

Use partial coverage, such as Max Coverage 0.35, to see the effect of Patch Size and Edge Detail.

Max Slope

  • Default 60°
  • Range 5° to 85°

Sets the steepest surface that holds snow. Lower values clear the snow from steep hillsides while keeping it on flatter ground.
