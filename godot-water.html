ScatterFlow documentation / Godot plugin

Godot: Water

Create water surfaces with animated waves, depth-based colour, refraction, and foam

Beta ScatterFlow Link for Godot is in early development. Features, settings, and interface elements may change between releases.

Water

Add ponds, lakes, oceans, pools, or swamps using configurable water presets. ScatterFlow provides waves, depth-based colour, caustics, and foam where the water meets surfaces.

Shallow areas remain clearer, while colour builds with depth. Waves distort the view beneath the surface, low viewing angles emphasize reflections, and caustics add moving light patterns to shallow ground.

Adding water

Point the 3D view at the target location and click the water-drop icon at the top of the dock. An SF Water node is created and selected, displaying its settings.

Set its height to the intended water level, then adjust Size X and Size Y in metres to cover the area. Terrain below that level becomes submerged, allowing a hill to form an island or a low area to become a lake.

Resolution: the number of surface points per metre. Higher values produce smoother large waves but increase processing cost.

Use Forward+ or Mobile. ScatterFlow water relies on scene depth and does not display correctly with the Compatibility renderer.

Presets

Preset includes Lake, Pond, Ocean, Tropical, Pool, Swamp, and River. These configure colour, clarity, waves, and caustics while preserving the water body's size and position. Start with the closest preset and refine it.

Look

Water Color: the colour reached in deeper water. Shallow regions remain clearer. Click the swatch to change the tint.

Turbidity (0–1): cloudiness. At 0, water is clear; higher values scatter more light and obscure the bottom sooner.

Roughness (0–1): reflection sharpness. Lower values produce sharper reflections; higher values soften them.

Caustics (0–4): the moving sunlight pattern beneath shallow water.

Foam: coverage along shorelines and objects intersecting the surface. Foam Width is measured in metres, with a default of 0.3. Increasing it to 1.2 m creates a wider shoreline band.

Clarity (default 1, range 0.1–4): a visibility multiplier relative to the preset. At 2, visibility depth doubles; 0.5 reduces it.

Refraction (default 1, range 0–3): distortion of the view beneath the waves. At 0, the bottom appears undistorted; higher values increase the movement in the image.

Whitecaps (default 1, range 0–2): foam on steep wave crests. It is most apparent with rougher water, such as the Ocean preset. See Waves for the wave controls.

Sun Through Waves (default 1, range 0–3): the glow of sunlight through wave crests when viewed toward the sun.

Waves

Wave Height and Wave Length, in metres, define wave height and spacing. Short, tall waves create choppy water; long, low waves create a gentler swell.

Wave Speed: the speed of wave travel.
Wave Direction: the travel direction in degrees.

Inspect rough water from a low angle when adjusting Whitecaps. If large waves look angular, increase Resolution to smooth the surface, balancing the added detail against performance.
