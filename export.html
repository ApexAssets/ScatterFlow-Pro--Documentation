Export

Transfer objects and scatter layers from Blender to Unreal Engine or Godot

How it works

ScatterFlow connects Blender directly to an open Unreal Engine or Godot project. Once connected, you can export supported scene content without manually exporting files and importing them in the engine.

The export handles two main types of content:

Objects, such as rocks, buildings, and terrain meshes, arrive with their materials.

Scatter layers arrive as editable ScatterFlow layers rather than thousands of separate objects. You can continue adjusting them in the engine.

Re-exporting updates existing content rather than creating duplicates. ScatterFlow tracks what has already been sent and updates changed content.

Install the engine plugin first. The destination project must be open with its ScatterFlow plugin available. This page covers Blender's export controls; see the Unreal and Godot guides for engine-side setup.

Step 1: Connect to your engine

Pick the engine and click Connect

1. Open the project in the engine with the ScatterFlow plugin enabled. In Unreal, click ScatterFlow in the main toolbar beside the Play controls. In Godot, open the ScatterFlow Pro dock.

2. Select Unreal or Godot at the top of Blender's Export tab.

3. Click Connect.

The status indicator shows the connection state:

Red, Not Connected: Blender has not connected to the engine.
Orange, Connecting…: a connection is being established.
Green, Connected to Unreal: the connection is ready for export.

Click Disconnect before switching to another engine.

Connection remains red? Check that the engine plugin is running. Then compare Link URL in Blender's ScatterFlow preferences under Export with the address shown in the engine's ScatterFlow panel.

Step 2: Choose what to send

Tick what you want to export

The two checkboxes under Export determine which content the export includes.

Selected Objects: exports objects selected in the viewport. The accompanying count distinguishes new objects, such as “2 new,” from “tracked” objects that have been sent before and can be updated.

Scatter System: exports enabled scatter layers. The count reports the number selected, such as “3 of 3.”

Use the checkboxes in Scatter Layers to choose individual layers. All enables every layer; None disables them all.

Excluding a layer does not delete it from the engine. It simply skips that layer's update. Exclude unchanged layers to reduce export work.

Step 3: Export

Click Export to Unreal

Click Export to Unreal or Export to Godot. The button displays Exporting… until the operation finishes.

Selected objects and enabled scatter layers can be sent in the same export. For example, export two manually placed boulders together with three scatter layers.

Continue editing in Blender and export again to update the engine. Previously exported objects are updated in place rather than duplicated.

Export settings

Click Settings beside the Export title to access optional export controls. The defaults provide a starting setup for most scenes.

What is sent along

For objects

Materials: includes the object's materials. Disable it when you intend to build the materials in the engine yourself.

Collision: adds collision so characters can stand on surfaces and are blocked by solid objects.

Nanite (Unreal only): enables Unreal's Nanite for exported objects, useful for detailed meshes.

For scatter layers

Emitter: includes the terrain used by the layers. Disable it when the terrain is already in the engine and only the layers need updating.

Nanite (Unreal only): enables Nanite for the terrain and scattered assets.

PCG Mode: sets the layer's initial behaviour in the engine. Exact Mode preserves Blender's instance positions. Procedural Mode lets the engine regenerate the scatter so you can adjust density and distribution there. You can change modes later in the engine.

What is sent, and what is not

Export supports the content listed below. Other parts of the environment need to be recreated or configured in the destination engine.

Sent to the engine

Selected mesh objects, with materials and optional collision. Materials are baked when required, as described under Texture Baking. Previously exported objects update in place.

Lights, except sun lamps. Collection instances, represented in Blender by an Empty that instances a collection, arrive with the collection's meshes and share one mesh per source object.

ScatterFlow scatter layers, including their instances. Layers above 1,000,000 instances, or 500,000 for Godot, use a stream file that the engine loads around the camera.

Ivy and water bodies created with ScatterFlow.

Blender objects configured to be visible only in shadows, with Camera off and Shadow on under Object Properties > Visibility, remain shadow-only in the engine. Objects disabled for rendering arrive hidden.

Not sent: set these up in the engine

The World: sky, HDRI, sky light, exposure, and view transform. Configure these in the engine, or use ScatterFlow Sky in Unreal.

Sun lamps and fog boxes: use the engine's sun and fog setup.

Weather: enable rain and snow through the engine's ScatterFlow controls.

Rivers: water bodies are supported, but river systems are not exported.

Not sent: other scattering

Only ScatterFlow layers are transferred as scatter systems. Particle systems, other Geometry Nodes setups, and instances from other scattering tools are excluded.

Convert them to real objects or bring them into ScatterFlow first. For GScatter environments, use the import workflow described under Biomes.

OpenVDB volume objects are also excluded and are reported in the export log.

Texture Baking

When your materials need converting

Blender material node setups do not always translate directly to the engine's material setup. ScatterFlow converts unsupported combinations, such as procedural textures, colour ramps, or mixed shaders, into image textures before export. This process is called baking.

Expand Texture Baking below the Export button. Its header shows the current behaviour: Auto, Bake All, or Off.

Bake on Export if not PBR: bakes only materials that require conversion. This is enabled by default and suits most scenes.

Bake All: bakes every material, including image-based materials. Use it when a transferred material differs from its Blender appearance.

Resolution: sets baked texture size. Source matches the source images. Select 2K or 4K for higher resolution, with larger output files.

Samples: increase this if baked textures appear grainy.

Timeout per Pass (min): limits the time allowed for one bake pass, such as Base Color on one object. If it exceeds the limit, the object is exported without baking. See When a bake is skipped or fails.

Initial exports can take longer. Baking runs during export. Later exports reuse cached images when the relevant inputs have not changed.

How baking works

This section explains how ScatterFlow decides what to bake, stores the results, and handles material features that cannot be preserved in a static texture.

Which materials are baked

A material consisting of image textures connected to a Principled BSDF can be transferred without baking. The engine rebuilds its supported material inputs from those textures.

Baking is used when the material cannot be reconstructed that way, including setups with:

procedural textures such as Noise, Voronoi, Musgrave, Wave, or Brick; Color Ramps; Mix Shaders; shaders other than Principled BSDF, such as Diffuse, Glossy, or Translucent; or node groups that modify their inputs.

Two kinds of setup can bypass baking despite including additional nodes:

Simple image adjustments, including Mapping-node tiling or offset, colour tinting, brightness or contrast changes, and roughness remapping. The texture and supported adjustments are transferred for the engine to apply.

Inactive operations, such as a Mix at factor 0, a Bump at strength 0, muted nodes, or groups with zero influence. ScatterFlow follows the active result through these nodes and exports the underlying textures.

What happens during a bake

ScatterFlow uses Cycles to bake material channels into separate images. Channels include Base Color, Roughness, Metallic, Specular, Opacity, and Normal, plus Translucency and Emission when present. Bump and normal-map inputs are combined into the baked normal map.

Baking uses a temporary copy of the object with its modifiers applied, in a separate background Blender process. Your active Blender session remains usable, and the scene's scatter is not loaded into the bake process. A failure in that process does not close the active session. The viewport progress card shows the current object and pass; press Esc to cancel.

The bake uses the GPU configured in Blender's Preferences > System, or the CPU when no GPU is configured. You can select the device under ScatterFlow's Preferences > Export > Texture Baking > Device. The panel reports when no GPU is set up.

Re-exports reuse the bakes

Images are cached in _BakeCache inside the export's Sync Folder, which is next to the .blend file by default. Re-exports reuse them unless the material, mesh UVs, or bake settings have changed.

Materials that depend only on UVs, including image mixes, ramps, colour adjustments, and normal maps, can share one bake across objects. For example, tree variants using the same UV-based leaf material reuse that material's baked textures.

Delete _BakeCache before exporting to force a complete rebake.

Ground materials that blend two textures

A terrain material may blend two tiling texture sets, such as grass and dirt, using a mask. Baking the whole terrain into one image can lose detail; for example, a 2K texture stretched across 100 m provides limited surface resolution.

ScatterFlow instead bakes each texture set as a tile and exports the blend mask separately. The engine tiles and blends the results. This requires each set to use one Mapping node without rotation, the first UV map, and no dependence on position or normals.

UV maps

Baking uses the object's first UV map, matching the map used by the engine.

Coordinates outside the 0–1 range are wrapped back into that range for baking, matching repeated textures such as tiled bark or planks.

UDIM materials are baked into a single image, causing their tiles to overlap. The export log warns when this occurs.

Complicated materials and node groups

Nested groups are supported, but some material effects depend on information that cannot be stored in a static image. The following sections describe the resulting limitations.

Node groups, nested groups and layered shaders

The bake follows connections through node groups and nested groups. You do not need to ungroup a material before exporting.

Layered materials, such as rock, moss, and dirt blended with masks, are baked using their combined result. Material Outputs inside groups, including those used by some vegetation assets, are also detected.

Nodes that depend on the camera or the lighting

Fresnel, Layer Weight, Light Path, Camera Data, and Ambient Occlusion can depend on the view or lighting. A bake captures a fixed result, so these baked effects do not continue responding to the camera in the engine.

Glass transparency driven by Fresnel or Layer Weight is a supported exception: ScatterFlow rebuilds it using the engine's Fresnel behaviour instead of baking the transparency effect.

Variation per copy or over time

Per-object variation: values from Object Info, such as Random or Location, or Particle Info can produce different colours for each copy in Blender. A baked image is shared by copies of the mesh, so this variation is not retained in the shared texture. Use separate mesh variants or the engine's per-instance variation controls.

Animation: time- or frame-driven material values are baked at the current frame. The resulting texture does not preserve the animation.

Displacement, volumes and weather

Displacement: Material Output displacement does not deform the exported engine mesh. Procedural displacement is baked into the normal map as apparent surface detail rather than geometry. An image height map connected to Material Output does not trigger baking by itself.

Volume shaders: not baked. Configure fog and other volume effects in the engine.

Rain ripples and wetness: ScatterFlow weather additions are excluded from material baking. The engine's weather system provides these effects live rather than fixing one animated state into a texture.

When a bake is skipped or fails

A bake failure does not stop the export. The affected material is sent with its available textures and values instead.

After export, the status bar summarizes the result, for example 12 material(s) baked, 1 mesh(es) skipped: Rock_03 (see Info log). The Info editor lists skipped objects and their reasons. For detailed bake output, open Window > Toggle System Console.

"no UV map"

The object has no UV map for the baked image. Its procedural materials are exported without baking.

Fix: select the object, enter Edit Mode with Tab, select all geometry, and use UV > Smart UV Project. Export again.

"invalid (NaN / infinite) coordinates"

The first UV map contains invalid values, potentially from an import or modifier.

Fix: unwrap the object again with UV > Smart UV Project or Unwrap. If a modifier creates the UVs, apply it and inspect the result in the UV Editor.

"bake timed out"

A pass exceeded Timeout per Pass. Baking stops for that object, and it is exported without the baked result.

Fix: increase Timeout per Pass when more processing time is needed. Dense meshes, 4K output, high Samples, and CPU baking can all increase bake time.

Bakes are very slow

GPU not configured: check Edit > Preferences > System > Cycles Render Devices. Configure a GPU rather than relying on CPU baking when appropriate for your setup.

Bake settings: Samples 4 is sufficient for most materials in this workflow. Source resolution preserves the size of existing textures. A 4K bake can take approximately four times as long as a 2K bake.

First export: materials that require baking have no cached results yet. Subsequent exports bake only changed content.

Pink or magenta textures

An image used by the material is missing, often after moving a scene between computers. ScatterFlow searches beside the .blend file and in its textures folder, and checks for another loaded copy.

Fix: use File > External Data > Find Missing Files, select the folder containing the textures, and export again.

Grainy, blurry or different from Blender

Grainy: increase Samples.

Blurry: increase Resolution. Source uses the largest image in the material, or 1K for fully procedural materials.

Different appearance: enable Bake All to bake rather than reconstruct the material from its textures. If differences remain, check the limitations under Complicated materials and node groups.

An object is not baked at all

Only mesh objects are baked. Curves, text, and other object types are exported without baking their materials.

Fix: convert the object with Object > Convert > Mesh. Use a copy when you need to preserve the editable original.

Player Preview

Walk through your scene

Player Preview lets you inspect the Blender scene from a player's viewpoint before exporting. It does not require a running engine or connection.

1. Position the viewport at the starting location.

2. Click Preview under Player Preview.

3. Navigate using the controls shown on the shortcut card:

W A S D moves, the mouse looks around, Shift runs, Space jumps, and Ctrl crouches.
V cycles between first-person, third-person, and top-down views.
The mouse wheel adjusts camera distance in third-person and top-down views.
Hold the right mouse button to zoom.
H hides the shortcut card. Esc ends the preview and restores the previous viewport.

Use the different views to inspect placement, scene scale, and how the environment reads while moving through it.

Open Settings below Preview to choose the starting camera and location, use the current viewport or 3D Cursor, enable full screen, adjust movement speeds and mouse sensitivity, or enable collision with scattered objects.

Slow viewport, slow character

Preview updates run up to 60 times per second and account for elapsed time, keeping movement speed consistent at rates such as 60 or 25 frames per second.

Each update accounts for at most one twentieth of a second. Below 20 frames per second, movement slows: at 10 frames per second it runs at half speed, and at 5 it runs at a quarter speed. Improve viewport performance rather than trying to compensate with Walk Speed.

What makes a scene slow to draw

Scatter density: large instance counts. Reduce Viewport Density or use Nano; see Optimization. These viewport changes preserve full render density.

Complex meshes: detailed assets repeated many times. Use lighter meshes or proxies during editing.

Modifiers: Subdivision Surface, Geometry Nodes, and other modifiers requiring recalculation.

Animation: armatures, drivers, simulations, animated objects, and wind.

Shading: demanding materials, lights, or textures in Material Preview or Rendered shading.

Other applications: an open game engine or another 3D application shares GPU resources.

Preview settings: enable Fullscreen Preview and leave Collide with Scattered Objects disabled for scenes with large instance counts.
